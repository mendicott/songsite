The Method, Briefly

What counts as a Rainbow song

Almost nothing sung at a Rainbow Gathering began there. The repertoire is a borrowed commons — fire-circle chants, kirtans, gospel rewrites, rounds and blessings — so a song is counted as a Rainbow song not by where it came from, but by whether the circles took it up. The working rule: a song belongs if it is attested at least twice, independently, with at least one attestation from a gathering itself — a songbook passed hand to hand there, or a field recording of it being sung. Songs carried purely in living memory enter through a first-person account from the person who carries them; those are the Living additions.

Choosing the Core 50

Popularity is judged from countable evidence rather than taste: how many independent songbooks print a song, how often it turns up across twenty-six years of field recordings, whether the community’s own compilations and song sites carry it, and how far it has travelled in years and geography. Songbook incidence weighs heaviest — someone chose to write a song down so it could be sung again. Where the evidence is thin, the index says so rather than guessing.

Where the melodies come from

The songbooks preserve the words and chords; the melodies are the endangered part. Each melody here is restored from the best evidence available for that song — the books’ occasional hand notation, published sheet music, or recordings of actual singing — and every page names its sources. Pages marked draft on the index are still being checked against those sources.

Rights

A few songs the circles love are under active copyright. They are honored by reference only — named on the index, their notation left with the rights holders.
