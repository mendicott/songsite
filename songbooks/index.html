Rainbow Songbook Collection

The source library behind the songs on this site: 39 independent songbooks gathered from Rainbow Gatherings, singing circles, medicine ceremonies, kirtans and ashrams worldwide. The books passed hand to hand and camp to camp, each one somebody’s labor of love — typed, hand-copied or hand-notated, photocopied and carried on. Each entry below shows a book’s cover, its size, and a short review of what it holds; a few of them preserve the rarest thing of all, staff notation of the melodies. The books are ordered by their share of the Top 100 — the ones that print the most of the core repertoire come first.

Alli Ruhi- Songs of the Rainbow.pdf

69 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle282 pages3.2 MB

One of the largest and best-organized Rainbow collections in the archive: one song per page with lyrics, chord letters and rhythm-slash patterns beneath. It opens with Rumi's "Come, come, whoever you are" and ranges across circle standards, pagan chants (May the Circle Be Open), Andean and Spanish medicine songs (Cuñaq), and a long run of Hindu bhajans and aartis. The clean text layer makes it excellent for searching, and the chord-plus-rhythm format is unusually performance-ready. It appears to be the text twin of the illustrated scan filed as Rainbow songs and prays.pdf, which shares its cover art.

Sound of The Heart.pdf

62 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle330 pages5.8 MB

A 330-page "heart singing" anthology whose preface lays out the most ambitious sectioning in the archive: Rainbow; Tribal (Native American, African, Aborigine, Nyabinghi, European folk, Gypsy, Hebrew, Flower Power, Bhajans); Beija Flor (Portuguese songs of the Amazonas); and Baladi (songs of the Middle East). Chords are given as slash-grid patterns below each song — a system that encodes strum rhythm as well as harmony. The Gypsy and Middle-Eastern sections (Otchi Chyornie, Romani songs transliterated from film soundtracks) cover ground no other book here touches. A major, eclectic source.

Rainbow Songs - Mantras, Bahjans & Rainbow Songs (Lyrics, Chords, YT links) Ebook.pdf

59 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle543 pages5.7 MB

"Rainbowsongs 1+2 — Ananda's Spiritual Songbook", the combined ebook edition of Ananda's two rainbowsongs.org volumes and, at over 500 songs, one of the broadest collections on the shelf: Sikh and Sanskrit mantras, bhajans, Hebrew and Christian songs, Taizé chants and the Rainbow circle standards (Above and Below, Circle Around, Earth My Body, Fly Like an Eagle) side by side, with a bilingual English/German preface. Its signature feature is that every song carries a link to a recording through the compiler's own redirect shortlinks, maintained so that when YouTube deletes a video the link is re-pointed rather than left dead — the only book in the archive that solves the dead-link problem structurally. Chords use European spelling (Am written as "a") with //: :// repeat marks; melodies are not notated, but the guaranteed recording per song makes it a strong melody-verification source.

Rainbow AU songs.pdf

57 of the Top 100Rainbow & circleStaff notationImage-only scan162 pages28.9 MB

The project's primary source. A 162-page scan of the two-volume French-compiled Rainbow songbook the whole melody-reconstruction effort is built on, with a handwritten index using "(p.) et (part)" partition markers and French titles like "Paix amour et lumière" alongside the international repertoire. Its greatest asset is the scarcest resource in the archive: hand-notated staff melodies for circle standards, page after page. It has no text layer, so it must be read by eye, and the booklet imposition clips some pages — but nearly every song page on this site traces back to this book. (A byte-identical copy, formerly filed as rainbow songbook (2).pdf, was consolidated 2026-09-18.)

Rainbow Caravan.pdf

49 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle225 pages7.3 MB

Compiled by ChuCha Rucha, this is the songbook of the Nomadic Rainbow Caravans: 325+ numbered songs "sung at the countless Rainbow Gatherings around the world since 1972," indexed by language and theme, with chords and English translations for the Spanish material. It is one of the highest-value books for the Core 50 — She's Been Waiting, One Planet Is Turning, May the Circle Be Open and many more appear here — and its closing "Nomads" section (I'm a Nomad) is unique caravan culture found nowhere else. A generous, sprawling, essential collection.

Rainbow songs and prays.pdf

49 of the Top 100Rainbow & circleImage-only scan340 pages34.0 MB

The largest file in the archive: a 340-page illustrated scan opening with the same psychedelic colored-pencil cover as Alli Ruhi- Songs of the Rainbow.pdf — this appears to be the full art-and-layout edition of that collection. Inside, songs are beautifully typeset with small chord letters floated above the syllables, organized into language sections marked by margin icons (the Hebrew section includes Im Betoch Ha'esh and Nuach with translations and singing notes). No text layer, so the searchable Alli Ruhi file is the working copy — but this is the definitive visual edition of the book, and the chord-over-syllable placement is more precise here.

Canciones.pdf

46 of the Top 100Medicine & icaros11 pages0.2 MB

An eleven-page cheat sheet that packs a remarkable amount in: alphabetized songs with inline chords, running from medicine standards (Ábrete Corazón, Above and Below) through Nick Barber's Celestial Heart to campfire reggae (Jammin') and What a Wonderful World. There is no title page, index or commentary — it reads like one guitarist's personal setlist compressed for printing. Useful precisely because it is a snapshot of what one circle actually sings, rather than an aspirational anthology. Its true identity surfaced in 2026: this is the "Family Songbook", version 1.1.3, compiled by "almoo" in December 2014 — two earlier revisions (v1.1.2 and an intermediate 1.1.3 draft, differing only in chord corrections) turned up and were consolidated 2026-09-18; this file is the compiler's final revision.

Mantra_Sammlung_Rafael.pdf

39 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantra77 pages1.2 MB

Rafael's German mantra collection: 79 pages of chants with capo positions, chords, and — its distinguishing feature — a short German explanation of each mantra's meaning and deity beneath the text. Beyond the Sanskrit core it wanders pleasantly into Hawaiian (He Kehau), Arabic (Mash'Allah) and English territory, including Rainbow standards like Above and Below and the Nahko-style Great Spirit. A good example of the German singing-circle tradition of annotating rather than just transcribing.

unilaiva-songbook.pdf

38 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle292 pages9.7 MB

The Finnish "Unilaiva songbook — paths to higher knowledge, By: humankind" (2019 beta, unilaiva.aavalla.net): the most professionally engineered book on the shelf, typeset in LaTeX with a numbered song system, per-song tag metadata, chord symbols set inline at the exact syllable, translations, and a full tag index (fire, circle, Divine Mother, even "chords missing"). Already assessed by the project — chords only, no melodies — it remains the gold standard for structure: its data model is essentially what the songsite's per-song JSON is reinventing. Compiled from Finnish medicine and Rainbow circles.

Rainbow Kirtan.pdf

33 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantra32 pages0.5 MB

The "Rainbow Kirtan Bhajan Mantra Heartsong Chant Book" (version 2014-01-12, compiler bkinsey), notable as one of the few explicitly licensed books in the archive — CC BY-NC-SA, stamped on every page. It is lyrics-only but cleverly indexed: an alphabetical contents plus thematic groupings (all the Krishna chants, all the Hanuman chants, Great Spirit songs) and even a word-level index at the back. Already mined by the project — a copy lives in notation-sources — it remains the best quick lookup for whether a chant belongs to the Rainbow kirtan canon.

Songs-of-Light.pdf

33 of the Top 100Regional & other traditions169 pages5.0 MB

"Songs of Light, Love and Devotion," compiled by Frank Otto (singularica.com), a Berlin singing-circle facilitator, current to 2024 — the newest actively-maintained book on the shelf, complete with links to his Telegram circle-recording channels and an invitation to submit corrections. Songs carry composer credits and capo/chord headers (Lasst uns Erinnern by "Carb — Colourful Tribe"), mixing German originals with the international repertoire, and ending, in true songbook fashion, with a comic German number about Bodo and his digger. Valuable as a living, versioned document of the Berlin scene.

song.book.pdf

31 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle20 pages1.0 MB

"Spiritual Song Book — New Age Songs of Devotion" by Daniela Ferdová: a compact Czech-made collection of circle standards (Above and Below, We Are a Circle, We Are Circling, We Are Opening, We Shall Lift Each Other Up) with chords above the lyrics, plus Spanish and reggae-flavored numbers. Its colophon is the best license statement on the shelf: "No rights are reserved. Any spreading of this book is highly recommended," in English and Czech. Small, cheerful, and squarely aimed at the same core repertoire as the project's manifest.

new song book.pdf

29 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle25 pages1.0 MB

A German-made Rainbow book with the most practical table of contents in the archive: songs are grouped "Good for Foodcircle" and "Good for Magic Hat" — organized by gathering ritual rather than by tradition. The Rainbow section (I Am Here to Release All of My Fears, How Could Anyone, Fire Sacred Fire) is followed by Sanskrit mantras sorted by deity energy with a short explanation of Hinduism, and it closes with the compiler's own songs, including a Chinese poem set to chords. Songbook Flora.pdf

29 of the Top 100Regional & other traditions110 pages1.4 MB

"Spirit-Wind: Spirit- & Herzenslieder, Medizin-Songs, Mantren" — a German circle book with a numbered index in three language sections. Its German section is the real treasure: twenty original German spirit songs (Frau Holle, Roter Mond überm Silbersee, Kranich-Lied) that exist in no other book on this shelf, alongside the international standards (Above and Below, Circle of Women) and Spanish medicine songs with German translations. A reminder that the European circles are generating their own repertoire, not just translating the American one.

Icaros.pdf

28 of the Top 100Medicine & icaros227 pages5.1 MB

The revised edition (v15.02.07) of Diego Palma's Ayahuasca-Wasi collection, expanded by a dozen songs over the version filed as Ayahuasca-Wasi song book.pdf. Everything that makes the earlier book good is here and slightly better: language-sorted indexes with capo and key for each song, composer attributions, and clean one-song-per-page layout with chord letters beside each line. Between the two, this is the copy to consult; the earlier one is worth keeping only to track how the repertoire evolved.

Booklet Songs from the Heart - Canciones del Corazon.pdf

27 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle38 pages1.0 MB

A charming DIY booklet by the Finnish traveler-musician Se Misha, complete with printing-and-folding instructions so you can bind your own copy. This 2017 edition of "Las Canciones del Corazones" is the largest, with some 140 songs: circle standards (With These Hands, Kuate Leno Leno), Brazilian Yemanjá songs, Sanskrit chants and a few Finnish touches, with chords listed at line ends in the compiler's own idiosyncratic system. Warm, personal, and clearly assembled from songs actually carried around the road-family circuit. (The two 2014 versions of the book, Spanish Songs.pdf from November, with about a hundred songs, and Canciones del Corrazon - Our Heart Songs.pdf from December, which adds five more under an illustrated cover, were consolidated 2026-09-19 and kept aside with the superseded editions. They hold thirteen songs this edition dropped, among them Madre Ayahuasca, Ojos del Cielo, Suddhossi Buddhossi and covers of Bob Marley, Radiohead and Beirut.)

french song book.pdf

22 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle109 pages4.3 MB

"Cercles de chants, Volume 1" (cerclesdechants.weebly.com) in its "version pour instrumentiste" — a French singing-circle book of about 154 numbered songs with chords. The mix is a faithful picture of a francophone circle: Rainbow standards (The River Is Flowing, We Are Opening Up, We Are One in Harmony), Sikh and Hindu mantras, Yoruba chants (Idé Weré), and genuinely French material (A tous mes frères, Prière pour l'eau) that appears nowhere else in the archive. Well-typeset and fully searchable; one of the better European compilations.

Mantra_s_rainbow_songs_iris-1.pdf

22 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle10 pages0.1 MB

A Dutch compiler's personal sheet titled "Mantra's – Rainbow – shaman songs – Hollandse Gouwe Ouwe" ("Dutch golden oldies"): 54 numbered items mixing Sanskrit mantras with Dutch glosses, Rainbow circle songs (We Are Opening Up, One in Harmony), Spanish gratitude songs, and a few purely Dutch originals like the hummingbird song "Kolibrie". The Gayatri mantra even gets a syllable-by-syllable esoteric exegesis in Dutch. Slight in size but a genuine document of how the repertoire is localized in the Netherlands.

Rainbow Songbook With Notes.pdf

22 of the Top 100Rainbow & circleStaff notationImage-only scan53 pages6.9 MB

A notation goldmine. Internally titled "minirainbowsongs", this scan delivers exactly what its file name promises: hand-inked staff notation, with chord letters and lyrics under the notes, for some seventy Rainbow standards — Fly Like an Eagle, We Are the Power in Everyone, I Am a Gypsy, Dear Friend, the Russian peace round Mir Miro Mir and more, in a confident calligraphic hand, closing with a Sanskrit section (four notated Hare Krishna settings) and German translations by "Thilo" throughout. In an archive where 35 of 39 books are words-and-chords only, actual melodies are the scarce resource, and every song in this book has one. There is no text layer, so it must be read by eye; a page-by-page song index now stands in for search. (A second, independent scan of the same book, filed as songbook.pdf — PDF author Maarten Vandekeybus, lacking the cover, foreword and index pages — was consolidated 2026-09-19 and kept aside for comparison: where the calligraphy is faint in one scan, the other may be clearer.)

Sol-Diamante-chords.pdf

22 of the Top 100Medicine & icaros416 pages18.1 MB

The archive's biggest book by page count: "Sound of Light DIAMANTE — Prayers, Affirmations, Sacred Songs of Healing and Joy," 416 pages with chords, under the epigraph "Sing from your soul, we are transforming all into light." The repertoire is new-paradigm medicine and light-language material (the visionary "Awaken" — Son of Sky, Daughter of the Earth), generously laid out one song per page with illustration. zangboekje 2023_2024.pdf

22 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantra44 pages0.3 MB

The 2023–2024 "Zangboek" of Dutch voice teacher Linnet van der Wal (linnetvanderwal.nl) — the newest dated book in the archive. Around ninety numbered chants with chords in clean uppercase layout: Sikh mantras (Aad Guray), the kirtan core (Durge Durge in Shantala's call-and-response setting, Jay Ambe), and contemporary circle songs like Sacred Space with its Yemaya coda. It closes with an Osho passage, in Dutch, on disappearing into the song — "you became like hollow bamboo, like a flute." A tidy snapshot of the current Dutch singing-circle repertoire.

Metta-Rainbow-Chantbook.pdf

20 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle24 pages0.2 MB

"Metta/Rainbowgem Chants, Kirtans, Bhajans, Heartsongs" (version 2011-01-02), the direct ancestor of the 2014 Rainbow Kirtan.pdf — same CC BY-NC-SA license stamped on every page, same alphabetical layout, half the size. What earns it a place of its own is a feature the later edition dropped: many chants carry a direct MP3 link into "Mettaben's Rainbow Chant Collection" on archive.org — Air Moves Us, Ancient Mother, We Are Opening Up and dozens more, each tied to an actual free recording. For a corpus where the recording is the melody's only witness, that makes this little 2011 book a melody-verification source out of proportion to its 24 pages; the project's mettaben_matches.csv cross-reference comes from exactly this collection.

Elf Songbook.pdf

19 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantra48 pages0.3 MB

The text edition of the ELF Spiritual Songbook: over ninety numbered chants, opening with the traditional Ganesha invocations and moving through Shiva, Devi and Sufi/Hebrew material (Ishq Allah, Od Yavo Shalom), each with chord letters and an English translation of the Sanskrit. Songbook2020pdf.pdf

19 of the Top 100Regional & other traditions71 pages0.8 MB

The Atlachinolli Meztli Moondance Austria songbook (moondanceaustria.com), prepared for the four-night Moondance ceremony of August 2021. It is the most ceremonially structured book in the archive: each song is tagged with symbols for element (fire, earth, wind, water) and ritual function (sweatlodge, circle, moon, sacred-pipe song), and the introduction explains that during the ceremony only the huehuetl drum and rattles accompany the voices. The repertoire blends Lakota and Nahuatl traditional songs with English circle material like Lindsey Wise's Gathering the Flowers and the Triple Goddess Chant.

IBIZA_CIRANDA.pdf

17 of the Top 100Regional & other traditions380 pages5.7 MB

The 2015 Ibiza "Ciranda — Songs & Mantras with chords", at 380 pages the second-largest book in the archive. It gathers the international ciranda repertoire — Brazilian hymns, mantras with meter markings (a 2/4 "Om Shiva Om / Shiva Hear My Call" in Dm, for instance), and heart songs — with chords and time signatures noted per song. The sheer breadth makes it a strong census source: many songs from the top-100 manifest turn up here in variant forms. Its 2017 successor is filed as IBIZA_05_SAGRADA_2017_web.pdf.

Songbook Dennis 03 2021 chords.pdf

16 of the Top 100Medicine & icaros86 pages0.5 MB

Dennis Ruemping's March 2021 personal songbook: medicine songs with meticulous headers — capo, meter and key for every song ("Capo II, 4/4, F#m") — plus English or German prose translations after each Spanish or Portuguese text. Includes core-50 material (Mother I Feel You, complete with heya vocable verses) alongside Nessi Gomez's All Related and Brazilian pontos. The original photocopied zine behind Elf Songbook.pdf: a scan of the spiral-bound "Elf Spiritual Songbook, Volume One" compiled and arranged by Baba Chris, with a collaged deity-and-devotee cover straight from the ashram photocopier. MasterMantra_v1.0.pdf

14 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantra128 pages2.1 MB

A large-print song-leader's book: one chant per page in big type with chords above the syllables and an "(all sing together)" cue under each title, clearly designed to be read across a circle or projected. Attributions are unusually consistent — Thai Circus, Yopi Jay, Deva Premal & Miten, Peia — which makes it handy for tracing who wrote the melodies the circles sing. RainbowChants.pdf

13 of the Top 100Rainbow & circleStaff notation10 pages6.1 MB

Twenty core chants in clean engraved staff notation. "Rainbow Chants that you might hear by a campfire at a Rainbow Gathering," compiled by Butterfly Bill of bliss-fire.com (2014): each chant is professionally typeset with the melody on the staff, chord letters above and lyrics under the notes — The Earth Is Our Mother, Fly Like an Eagle, We All Come from the Goddess, We Are Opening and more, straight from the heart of the Core 50. A rough scan of a photocopied gathering songbook from 2005 — handwritten and typewritten pages, with an OCR layer so garbled it is almost decorative ("Respect the Music" becomes scattered letters). The full illustrated songbook of "Rainbow Spirit: From the Heart of the Fire" — the 1998 Conscious Wave project whose bare lyric sheet is filed as Rainbow Spirit songs.pdf. Rainbow Parody songs.pdf

3 of the Top 100Rainbow & circle84 pages0.3 MB

"The Alternative Rainbow Songbook", 2nd edition, by the travel writer Tom Thumb — the archive's only comedy volume. The introduction is a small essay on learning to love the Rainbow by laughing at it ("God is a comedian, playing to an audience too afraid to laugh"), followed by affectionate parodies of the very songs the rest of this shelf takes seriously. Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Full ©.pdf

3 of the Top 100Kirtan & mantraMelody letters97 pages0.7 MB

Natavar Knudsen's ©2020 chantbook, the master volume of the seven-file Shanti Salish set, and the archive's most unusual notation system: every chant is written as letter-name melody sequences aligned over the syllables, with the drone note specified and a link to the source recording (Krishna Das, Kripalu, Deva Premal, Jai Uttal) for each. It is, in effect, machine-readable melodic contour without staff notation — pitch classes but no rhythm. For any kirtan in the manifest, this book can seed a melody spec that the recording then confirms. Its six companion deity volumes, which split the same material by deity, were retired from the collection 2026-09-19.

The v18.02 edition of Diego Palma's Sacred Valley Tribe "Songbook ESPAÑOL & OTROS" — the current Spanish volume of the series, superseding the v17.07 copy now filed under superseded as Sacred valley1 spanish and other.pdf.
