Rainbow Songbook Shelf

The consolidated RainbowSongBooks archive: 48 independent PDF songbooks gathered from Rainbow Gatherings, singing circles, medicine ceremonies, kirtans and ashrams worldwide, plus 3 superseded editions quarantined in superseded\ (deduplicated 2026-09-16 and -17; rainbow-rising-spirit-of-the-rainbows.pdf, a zine rather than a songbook, removed 2026-09-17). Each entry shows the exact file name, a title-page crop, and a short review. Two discoveries from this survey: Rainbow Songbook With Notes.pdf and songbook.pdf both contain hand-inked staff notation, and rainbow songbook (2).pdf is an alternate scan of the project's primary source, Rainbow AU songs.pdf.

Alli Ruhi- Songs of the Rainbow.pdf

Rainbow & circle282 pages3.2 MB

One of the largest and best-organized Rainbow collections in the archive: one song per page with lyrics, chord letters and rhythm-slash patterns beneath. It opens with Rumi's "Come, come, whoever you are" and ranges across circle standards, pagan chants (May the Circle Be Open), Andean and Spanish medicine songs (Cuñaq), and a long run of Hindu bhajans and aartis. The clean text layer makes it excellent for searching, and the chord-plus-rhythm format is unusually performance-ready. It appears to be the text twin of the illustrated scan filed as Rainbow songs and prays.pdf, which shares its cover art.

aolbhajans.pdf

Kirtan & mantra68 pages0.1 MB

A tidy Word-typeset collection of Art of Living satsang bhajans in the tradition of Sri Sri Ravi Shankar, with a full table of contents and — its most useful feature — multiple chord arrangements ("Ver 1", "Ver 2") for many chants, showing how the same bhajan is harmonized differently in practice. The repertoire is purely Hindu devotional (Om Namah Shivaya, Devi bhajans, guru songs) with essentially no Rainbow-specific material, but it is a solid cross-reference for the mantra half of the corpus.

superseded\Ayahuasca-Wasi song book.pdf

Medicine & icarosSuperseded edition216 pages4.1 MB

Diego Palma's Ayahuasca-Wasi ceremony book (version 14.05.23 from sacredvalleytribe.com): medicine songs, mantras, icaros and a-cappella songs, indexed by language with capo position and key for every entry — a professional touch few books in the archive match. Attributions name the composers (Shimshai, Alonso del Río, Jarah Tree, Darpan), which makes it valuable for rights research as well as repertoire. This is an earlier edition of the collection whose 15.02.07 revision is filed as Icaros.pdf.

Booklet Songs from the Heart - Canciones del Corazon.pdf

Rainbow & circle38 pages1.0 MB

A charming DIY booklet by the Finnish traveler-musician Se Misha, complete with printing-and-folding instructions so you can bind your own copy. The songs mix English circle standards (With These Hands, Kuate Leno Leno), Brazilian Yemanjá songs, Sanskrit chants and a few Finnish touches, with chords listed at line ends in the compiler's own idiosyncratic system. Warm, personal, and clearly assembled from songs actually carried around the road-family circuit; a shorter Spanish-leaning sibling is filed as Spanish Songs.pdf.

Canciones.pdf

Medicine & icaros11 pages0.2 MB

An eleven-page cheat sheet that packs a remarkable amount in: alphabetized songs with inline chords, running from medicine standards (Ábrete Corazón, Above and Below) through Nick Barber's Celestial Heart to campfire reggae (Jammin') and What a Wonderful World. There is no title page, index or commentary — it reads like one guitarist's personal setlist compressed for printing. Useful precisely because it is a snapshot of what one circle actually sings, rather than an aspirational anthology.

Ciranda PoimPoim.pdf

Regional & other traditions234 pages2.8 MB

A Brazilian ciranda hymnal from Angel Heart Publishing: numbered Portuguese devotional hymns in the Santo Daime style (Firmeza, prayers to Jesus, Maria and the forest), presented as clean lyrics without chords. The opening pages are decorative images, but the body has a good text layer. It documents the Brazilian hinário tradition that flows into European ciranda circles, and pairs naturally with the two Ibiza volumes; for the melody project it is lyrics-only reference material.

Elf Songbook.pdf

Kirtan & mantra48 pages0.3 MB

The text edition of the ELF Spiritual Songbook: over ninety numbered chants, opening with the traditional Ganesha invocations and moving through Shiva, Devi and Sufi/Hebrew material (Ishq Allah, Od Yavo Shalom), each with chord letters and an English translation of the Sanskrit. A closing page explains the compiler's chord conventions (lowercase for minor, markers for repeated progressions). Dense and unglamorous, but one of the most complete kirtan references in the archive — and searchable, unlike its scanned sibling below.

Elf_Spiritual_Songbook_Vol_One_Compiled_Arranged_by_Baba_Chris.pdf

Kirtan & mantraImage-only scan44 pages31.5 MB

The original photocopied zine behind Elf Songbook.pdf: a scan of the spiral-bound "Elf Spiritual Songbook, Volume One" compiled and arranged by Baba Chris, with a collaged deity-and-devotee cover straight from the ashram photocopier. Inside are the same numbered chants in typewritten pages decorated with calligraphy and Islamic and Hindu ornaments. There is no text layer, so it can't be searched, but as an artifact it preserves the look and feel of a real hand-assembled gathering songbook better than almost anything else on this shelf.

french song book.pdf

Rainbow & circle109 pages4.3 MB

"Cercles de chants, Volume 1" (cerclesdechants.weebly.com) in its "version pour instrumentiste" — a French singing-circle book of about 154 numbered songs with chords. The mix is a faithful picture of a francophone circle: Rainbow standards (The River Is Flowing, We Are Opening Up, We Are One in Harmony), Sikh and Hindu mantras, Yoruba chants (Idé Weré), and genuinely French material (A tous mes frères, Prière pour l'eau) that appears nowhere else in the archive. Well-typeset and fully searchable; one of the better European compilations.

IBIZA_05_SAGRADA_2017_web.pdf

Regional & other traditions248 pages2.0 MB

"Sagrada Ibiza — Songs & Mantras 2017", the later and more polished of the two Ibiza ciranda books, dedicated "to the family of eagle and condor... the medicine bearers & music makers." Nearly 250 pages of songs with chords spanning Portuguese cirandas, English heart songs, French rounds and mantras, professionally laid out one song per page. Together with IBIZA_CIRANDA.pdf it documents the Ibiza medicine-music scene; the 2017 volume has the cleaner typography of the pair.

IBIZA_CIRANDA.pdf

Regional & other traditions380 pages5.7 MB

The 2015 Ibiza "Ciranda — Songs & Mantras with chords", at 380 pages the second-largest book in the archive. It gathers the international ciranda repertoire — Brazilian hymns, mantras with meter markings (a 2/4 "Om Shiva Om / Shiva Hear My Call" in Dm, for instance), and heart songs — with chords and time signatures noted per song. The sheer breadth makes it a strong census source: many songs from the top-100 manifest turn up here in variant forms. Its 2017 successor is filed as IBIZA_05_SAGRADA_2017_web.pdf.

Icaros.pdf

Medicine & icaros227 pages5.1 MB

The revised edition (v15.02.07) of Diego Palma's Ayahuasca-Wasi collection, expanded by a dozen songs over the version filed as Ayahuasca-Wasi song book.pdf. Everything that makes the earlier book good is here and slightly better: language-sorted indexes with capo and key for each song, composer attributions, and clean one-song-per-page layout with chord letters beside each line. Between the two, this is the copy to consult; the earlier one is worth keeping only to track how the repertoire evolved.

Mahadevi-Ashram-Bhajans&Mantras-Booklet.pdf

Kirtan & mantra16 pages0.8 MB

A graceful sixteen-page booklet from Mahadevi Ashram ("Love · Evolve · Awaken"), containing about sixty numbered bhajans and mantras with lowercase-minor chord letters and unusually good English translations — the Sanskrit invocations are rendered with real care rather than boilerplate. It opens with a full Devi invocation and closes with the traditional Sanskrit peace prayers, translated. Small but dense, and the most elegant of the pure-kirtan booklets in the archive.

Mantra_Sammlung_Rafael.pdf

Kirtan & mantra77 pages1.2 MB

Rafael's German mantra collection: 79 pages of chants with capo positions, chords, and — its distinguishing feature — a short German explanation of each mantra's meaning and deity beneath the text. Beyond the Sanskrit core it wanders pleasantly into Hawaiian (He Kehau), Arabic (Mash'Allah) and English territory, including Rainbow standards like Above and Below and the Nahko-style Great Spirit. A good example of the German singing-circle tradition of annotating rather than just transcribing.

Mantra_s_rainbow_songs_iris-1.pdf

Rainbow & circle10 pages0.1 MB

A Dutch compiler's personal sheet titled "Mantra's – Rainbow – shaman songs – Hollandse Gouwe Ouwe" ("Dutch golden oldies"): 54 numbered items mixing Sanskrit mantras with Dutch glosses, Rainbow circle songs (We Are Opening Up, One in Harmony), Spanish gratitude songs, and a few purely Dutch originals like the hummingbird song "Kolibrie". The Gayatri mantra even gets a syllable-by-syllable esoteric exegesis in Dutch. Slight in size but a genuine document of how the repertoire is localized in the Netherlands.

MasterMantra_v1.0.pdf

Kirtan & mantra128 pages2.1 MB

A large-print song-leader's book: one chant per page in big type with chords above the syllables and an "(all sing together)" cue under each title, clearly designed to be read across a circle or projected. Attributions are unusually consistent — Thai Circus, Yopi Jay, Deva Premal & Miten, Peia — which makes it handy for tracing who wrote the melodies the circles sing. The repertoire leans kirtan (Hey Ganga Ma, Jay Ma, Om Namo Narayanaya) with some English heart songs. Version 1.0 of what was evidently meant to be a living document.

superseded\medicine_songbook-english.pdf

Medicine & icarosSuperseded edition130 pages2.2 MB

The Sacred Valley Tribe "Songbook ENGLISH" v16.04 by Diego Palma — an earlier edition of the English volume whose v19.09 revision is filed as sacred valley2 song book english.pdf. The format is the series' best feature: every song gets capo position, chords aligned to lyric syllables, and a rhythm pattern spelled out phonetically ("tumchá • tumchá takitúm"), with occasional notes on a song's origin such as the Metta Bhavana prayer behind "May All Beings". Superseded by v19.09 but useful for tracking which songs entered the canon when.

new song book.pdf

Rainbow & circle25 pages1.0 MB

A German-made Rainbow book with the most practical table of contents in the archive: songs are grouped "Good for Foodcircle" and "Good for Magic Hat" — organized by gathering ritual rather than by tradition. The Rainbow section (I Am Here to Release All of My Fears, How Could Anyone, Fire Sacred Fire) is followed by Sanskrit mantras sorted by deity energy with a short explanation of Hinduism, and it closes with the compiler's own songs, including a Chinese poem set to chords. (Its content-identical twin, formerly filed as rainbow medicine songboko.pdf, was removed 2026-09-17.)

Rainbow Caravan.pdf

Rainbow & circle225 pages7.3 MB

Compiled by ChuCha Rucha, this is the songbook of the Nomadic Rainbow Caravans: 325+ numbered songs "sung at the countless Rainbow Gatherings around the world since 1972," indexed by language and theme, with chords and English translations for the Spanish material. It is one of the highest-value books for the Core 50 — She's Been Waiting, One Planet Is Turning, May the Circle Be Open and many more appear here — and its closing "Nomads" section (I'm a Nomad) is unique caravan culture found nowhere else. A generous, sprawling, essential collection.

Rainbow Kirtan.pdf

Kirtan & mantra32 pages0.5 MB

The "Rainbow Kirtan Bhajan Mantra Heartsong Chant Book" (version 2014-01-12, compiler bkinsey), notable as one of the few explicitly licensed books in the archive — CC BY-NC-SA, stamped on every page. It is lyrics-only but cleverly indexed: an alphabetical contents plus thematic groupings (all the Krishna chants, all the Hanuman chants, Great Spirit songs) and even a word-level index at the back. Already mined by the project — a copy lives in notation-sources — it remains the best quick lookup for whether a chant belongs to the Rainbow kirtan canon.

Rainbow Parody songs.pdf

Rainbow & circle84 pages0.3 MB

"The Alternative Rainbow Songbook", 2nd edition, by the travel writer Tom Thumb — the archive's only comedy volume. The introduction is a small essay on learning to love the Rainbow by laughing at it ("God is a comedian, playing to an audience too afraid to laugh"), followed by affectionate parodies of the very songs the rest of this shelf takes seriously. Zero value for melody reconstruction, since the tunes are the originals, but real value as cultural commentary — and as proof that the standards were so universally known they could be parodied.

rainbow songbook (2).pdf

Rainbow & circleStaff notationImage-only scan162 pages28.9 MB

This is an alternate scan of the project's primary source. Its 162 pages and handwritten index — with the telltale "(p.) et (part)" partition markers and French titles like "Paix amour et lumière" — match Rainbow AU songs.pdf, the two-volume French-compiled book the whole melody-reconstruction effort is built on. That makes this file quietly one of the most important on the shelf: where the primary scan is blurry or clipped by the booklet imposition, this second copy may be legible. It has no text layer, but every notation page in the primary book exists here too. Worth a systematic side-by-side pass.

Rainbow Songbook With Notes.pdf

Rainbow & circleStaff notationImage-only scan53 pages6.9 MB

A notation goldmine. Internally titled "minirainbowsongs", this scan delivers exactly what its file name promises: hand-inked staff notation, with chord letters and lyrics under the notes, for Rainbow standards — Fly Like an Eagle, We Are the Power in Everyone and more, in a confident calligraphic hand. In an archive where 47 of 55 books are words-and-chords only, actual melodies are the scarce resource, and this book has them on page after page. No text layer, so it must be read by eye, but it belongs at the front of the queue for cross-checking the Core 50 encodes.

Rainbow songs and prays.pdf

Rainbow & circleImage-only scan340 pages34.0 MB

The largest file in the archive: a 340-page illustrated scan opening with the same psychedelic colored-pencil cover as Alli Ruhi- Songs of the Rainbow.pdf — this appears to be the full art-and-layout edition of that collection. Inside, songs are beautifully typeset with small chord letters floated above the syllables, organized into language sections marked by margin icons (the Hebrew section includes Im Betoch Ha'esh and Nuach with translations and singing notes). No text layer, so the searchable Alli Ruhi file is the working copy — but this is the definitive visual edition of the book, and the chord-over-syllable placement is more precise here.

Rainbow Spirit songs.pdf

Rainbow & circle6 pages0.7 MB

The lyric sheet for "Rainbow Spirit: Twenty-Seven Songs and Chants Celebrating the Spirit of the Rainbow Family of Living Light" — the 1998 Conscious Wave video whose audio circulated free as MP3s, laid out for PDF by Allen Butcher of the Denver chant circles. Only six pages, but historically significant: these 27 songs were, in effect, a canonized 1998 snapshot of the core Rainbow repertoire, with recordings to match. (A pixel-identical duplicate, formerly filed as Rainbow songbook.pdf, was removed from the archive 2026-09-17.)

Sacred valley1 spanish and other.pdf

Medicine & icaros179 pages6.7 MB

Diego Palma's Sacred Valley Tribe "Songbook ESPAÑOL & OTROS" v17.07: the Spanish-language volume of the series, from A Li Kuni and Ábrete Corazón through Cuñaq to the wistful closing "Paula". The house format — capo, syllable-aligned chords, phonetic rhythm patterns — is applied to the whole Andean and Amazonian repertoire, and asterisks mark songs added in this revision. (An identical copy, formerly filed as Songbook-ESPANOL.pdf, was removed 2026-09-17.)

sacred valley2 song book english.pdf

Medicine & icaros152 pages6.5 MB

The newest English volume in the archive's Sacred Valley Tribe set (v19.09), superseding the v16.04 copy filed as medicine_songbook-english.pdf with about twenty added songs, marked by asterisks in the index (Ancient Mother II, Breath of Life, Give Praise to the Sun). Relevant to the Core 50 rights questions: it prints "Long Time Sun" credited as an original by Melita rather than the Incredible String Band lineage, an attribution worth noting in the provenance file. As throughout the series, chords, capo and rhythm patterns are exact; melodies are not notated.

sacred valley3 mantras.pdf

Kirtan & mantra137 pages4.4 MB

The v19.09 MANTRAS volume of the Sacred Valley Tribe series — the current edition of the book whose v17.07 printing is filed as Songbook-MANTRAS.pdf. Where most mantra books give one version per chant, Palma's index lists variants by melody source: three different Hare Krishnas (Alonso, Lakshmi, Maneesh), two Asato Maas (Claire, Deva), two Green Taras. That variant-consciousness is exactly the mindset of the melody-reconstruction project, making this the most methodologically sympathetic mantra book on the shelf. Closes with a Yogi Bhajan quote on gratitude.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Full ©.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters97 pages0.7 MB

Natavar Knudsen's ©2020 chantbook, the master volume of the seven-file Shanti Salish set, and the archive's most unusual notation system: every chant is written as letter-name melody sequences aligned over the syllables, with the drone note specified and a link to the source recording (Krishna Das, Kripalu, Deva Premal, Jai Uttal) for each. It is, in effect, machine-readable melodic contour without staff notation — pitch classes but no rhythm. For any kirtan in the manifest, this book can seed a melody spec that the recording then confirms. The six companion files below split the same material by deity.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Ganesh ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters14 pages0.3 MB

The Ganesha fascicle of the Shanti Salish set, opening with a well-written page on who Ganesha is and why kirtans traditionally begin with him, then giving a dozen-plus Ganesha chants (Ganesha Sharanam, Om Gam Ganapataye in many settings) in the letter-note-over-syllable system with drones and source-recording links for each — Kripalu recordings, Dave Stringer, Edo & Jo, MC Yogi. The deity essay plus multiple melodic settings of the same mantra text makes this little booklet a model of how variant melodies should be documented.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Guru_Other ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters18 pages0.2 MB

The catch-all fascicle of the Shanti Salish set: guru chants, opening and closing prayers (Gayatri, Asato Ma, Lokah Samastah in several settings), Sufi chants and English-language material that fits no single deity chapter — including Rickie Beckwith's "We Let the Light Wash Over Us" with its melody spelled out in letter names. Like its siblings it pairs every chant with a drone note and a link to the recording it transcribes. The prayers section is the most practically useful: these are the chants that open and close nearly every kirtan.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Krishna ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters58 pages0.9 MB

At 58 pages the largest deity fascicle in the Shanti Salish set, reflecting Krishna's dominance of the kirtan repertoire: Maha Mantra settings from traditional to Krishna Das, Radhe chants, Govinda chants, Narayana chants — each with letter-name melody, drone, and a link to the exact recording transcribed. It opens with a readable essay on Krishna's story explaining why so many chant names (Govinda, Gopala, Murari) refer to him. For reconstructing any Hare Krishna variant in the corpus this is the first place to look.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Ma ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters25 pages0.5 MB

The Divine Mother fascicle: Kali, Durga, Lakshmi and Jagadambe chants in the Shanti Salish letter-note system, opening with a clear primer on the Tridevi and the many names under which the Goddess is invoked. Directly relevant to the Core 50 — "Kali Durge Namo Namah" and the Jai Ma family are here in multiple melodic settings (Kripalu, Krishna Das, Brenda McMorrow), each tied to its source recording — and it even strays ecumenically into a Mary Magdalene chant by Gina Sala. The census hit "Jay Ma" (30 books) can be melody-checked against this volume.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Ram ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters32 pages0.2 MB

The Ram fascicle covers the Rama/Sita/Hanuman chant family — Rama Bolo, Shri Ram Jaya Ram (in Kripalu, Krishna Das and Dave Stringer settings), Om Ram Ramaya Swaha — with the set's usual letter-name melodies, drones and recording links, prefaced by a short essay on the Ramayana and why Ram stands for dharma. Notable inclusion: Karen Drucker's English "You Are the Heart" paired with Natavar's own Sanskrit response verse, an example of the East-West blending typical of the Salish Sea kirtan scene the series is named for.

Shanti Salish Kirtan Chantbook - Shiva ©2020.pdf

Kirtan & mantraMelody letters26 pages0.5 MB

The Shiva fascicle: Om Namah Shivaya in its many melodic lives (Krishna Das's Live on Earth setting among them), Hara Hara Mahadeva, Jaya Shiva Shankara and Samba Sadashiva, all in letter-note melody with drones and source links, introduced by a Nataraja essay quoting Yogananda. Since "Om Shiva Om" and the Shiva-chant family scored high in the corpus census, this volume is a ready-made melodic cross-reference for them. With this file the seven-part Shanti Salish set is complete — collectively the archive's second notation system after staff.

Sol-Diamante-chords.pdf

The archive's biggest book by page count: "Sound of Light DIAMANTE — Prayers, Affirmations, Sacred Songs of Healing and Joy," 416 pages with chords, under the epigraph "Sing from your soul, we are transforming all into light." The repertoire is new-paradigm medicine and light-language material (the visionary "Awaken" — Son of Sky, Daughter of the Earth), generously laid out one song per page with illustration. "Spiritual Song Book — New Age Songs of Devotion" by Daniela Ferdová: a compact Czech-made collection of circle standards (Above and Below, We Are a Circle, We Are Circling, We Are Opening, We Shall Lift Each Other Up) with chords above the lyrics, plus Spanish and reggae-flavored numbers. The archive's second staff-notation discovery. songbook2005.pdf

A rough scan of a photocopied gathering songbook from 2005 — handwritten and typewritten pages, with an OCR layer so garbled it is almost decorative ("Respect the Music" becomes scattered letters). Fifty-three numbered songs with a handwritten index: Hebrew songs (Mode Ani, Egel Tal), German ones (Alles verändert sich), reggae (Babylon Is Fallin'), and Rainbow items like Welcome Home and May the Circle Be Open. Songbook Dennis 03 2021 chords.pdf

Medicine & icaros86 pages0.5 MB

Dennis Ruemping's March 2021 personal songbook: medicine songs with meticulous headers — capo, meter and key for every song ("Capo II, 4/4, F#m") — plus English or German prose translations after each Spanish or Portuguese text. Includes core-50 material (Mother I Feel You, complete with heya vocable verses) alongside Nessi Gomez's All Related and Brazilian pontos. Songbook Flora.pdf

"Spirit-Wind: Spirit- & Herzenslieder, Medizin-Songs, Mantren" — a German circle book with a numbered index in three language sections. Its German section is the real treasure: twenty original German spirit songs (Frau Holle, Roter Mond überm Silbersee, Kranich-Lied) that exist in no other book on this shelf, alongside the international standards (Above and Below, Circle of Women) and Spanish medicine songs with German translations. The v17.07 edition of the Sacred Valley Tribe MANTRAS volume — superseded by the v19.09 copy filed as sacred valley3 mantras.pdf, which adds a dozen chants (Gate Gate, Hey Amba, Baba Hanuman variants are marked new there). Songbook2020pdf.pdf

Regional & other traditions71 pages0.8 MB

The Atlachinolli Meztli Moondance Austria songbook (moondanceaustria.com), prepared for the four-night Moondance ceremony of August 2021. It is the most ceremonially structured book in the archive: each song is tagged with symbols for element (fire, earth, wind, water) and ritual function (sweatlodge, circle, moon, sacred-pipe song), and the introduction explains that during the ceremony only the huehuetl drum and rattles accompany the voices. The repertoire blends Lakota and Nahuatl traditional songs with English circle material like Lindsey Wise's Gathering the Flowers and the Triple Goddess Chant.

songbookneu.pdf

Rainbow & circle9 pages0.06 MB

"Songs of the Heart" (May 2016): the smallest file in the archive at 60 KB, a nine-page LaTeX-clean sheet of about forty heart songs with chords, indexed alphabetically. Despite its size it holds several manifest songs — Long Time Sun, Evening Rise, How Can Anyone (sic), We Shall Lift Each Other Up — plus German items like Wanderer im Weltgetriebe. German-made ("Licht durchflutet", "songbookneu" = "new songbook"), it is the archive's best example of the minimal, no-frills chord sheet that actually travels to gatherings in backpacks.

Songs-of-Light.pdf

Regional & other traditions169 pages5.0 MB

"Songs of Light, Love and Devotion," compiled by Frank Otto (singularica.com), a Berlin singing-circle facilitator, current to 2024 — the newest actively-maintained book on the shelf, complete with links to his Telegram circle-recording channels and an invitation to submit corrections. Songs carry composer credits and capo/chord headers (Lasst uns Erinnern by "Carb — Colourful Tribe"), mixing German originals with the international repertoire, and ending, in true songbook fashion, with a comic German number about Bodo and his digger. Valuable as a living, versioned document of the Berlin scene.

Sound of The Heart.pdf

Rainbow & circle330 pages5.8 MB

A 330-page "heart singing" anthology whose preface lays out the most ambitious sectioning in the archive: Rainbow; Tribal (Native American, African, Aborigine, Nyabinghi, European folk, Gypsy, Hebrew, Flower Power, Bhajans); Beija Flor (Portuguese songs of the Amazonas); and Baladi (songs of the Middle East). Chords are given as slash-grid patterns below each song — a system that encodes strum rhythm as well as harmony. The Gypsy and Middle-Eastern sections (Otchi Chyornie, Romani songs transliterated from film soundtracks) cover ground no other book here touches. A major, eclectic source.

Spanish Songs.pdf

Rainbow & circle22 pages0.3 MB

Se Misha's "Las Canciones del Corazones" in its denser, Spanish-leaning form — the same Finnish compiler and front matter as Booklet Songs from the Heart, but with a different and larger song list packed into 22 pages: an alphabetical index of some eighty items from Ábrete Corazón and El Pescador to Bremenskie Muzykanty (Russian!), cumbia and lambada alongside the mantras. Where the Booklet is designed for printing and folding, this reads as the compiler's fuller working file. Between the two, this one has more repertoire; the other has more charm.

taizechords.pdf

Regional & other traditions16 pages0.3 MB

A 2009 guitar-chord companion to the chants of the Taizé community, its title page listing "Songs from" in nineteen languages — a nice emblem of the ecumenical tradition it serves. Some sixty numbered chants (Dans nos obscurités, Ubi Caritas, Nada te turbe, Veni Sancte Spiritus) are given with syllable-aligned chord letters, including German-notation quirks (H for B natural) and capo alternatives. The one Christian-contemplative book on the shelf; its ostinato-round form is a direct ancestor of the short repetitive circle-song style the Rainbow repertoire favors.

Turkish Sufi Songbook.pdf

Regional & other traditions178 pages3.4 MB

"Tasawwuf İlahiler" of the Rifa'i Marufi order (January 2021): Turkish Sufi hymns with serious musicological apparatus — each ilahi names its makam (Uşşak, etc.), its poet (Yunus Emre) and composer, and its position in the ceremonial set ("preceded by... followed by..."). It opens with a primer on the principles of Sufism and the Al-Fatiha with English translation, and closes with a dedication to Şerif Baba Çatalkaya. Entirely outside the Rainbow repertoire, but the most scholarly book in the archive and a model of how oral melodic tradition can be documented without staff notation.

The Finnish "Unilaiva songbook — paths to higher knowledge, By: humankind" (2019 beta, unilaiva.aavalla.net): the most professionally engineered book on the shelf, typeset in LaTeX with a numbered song system, per-song tag metadata, chord symbols set inline at the exact syllable, translations, and a full tag index (fire, circle, Divine Mother, even "chords missing"). Already assessed by the project — chords only, no melodies — it remains the gold standard for structure: its data model is essentially what the songsite's per-song JSON is reinventing. Compiled from Finnish medicine and Rainbow circles.

The 2023–2024 "Zangboek" of Dutch voice teacher Linnet van der Wal (linnetvanderwal.nl) — the newest dated book in the archive. Around ninety numbered chants with chords in clean uppercase layout: Sikh mantras (Aad Guray), the kirtan core (Durge Durge in Shantala's call-and-response setting, Jay Ambe), and contemporary circle songs like Sacred Space with its Yemaya coda. It closes with an Osho passage, in Dutch, on disappearing into the song — "you became like hollow bamboo, like a flute." A tidy snapshot of the current Dutch singing-circle repertoire.
